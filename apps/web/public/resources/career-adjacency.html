You Don't Always Need to Start Over: The Power of Career Adjacency

How existing skills can become a bridge to another career.

Career Change 6 mi read

One of the biggest fears about changing careers is:

"I'll have to start all over again."

Often, you don't.

Your existing skills may already be useful in another career. The challenge is identifying which skills transfer and which additional skills you need.

Lightcast's report calls this skills adjacency.

What is career adjacency?

Two careers may look very different by title but share substantial skills.

Lightcast uses Electrical Engineer → Solar Engineer as an example. The report identifies transitional skills including Solar Engineering, Solar Design, PVsyst and Energy Production.

The key idea is:

Don't ask only how different two careers are. Ask how much of the journey you already know.

Your existing experience has value

A software professional considering Product Management may already have technical understanding, problem-solving ability, software-delivery experience and knowledge of users and requirements.

They may need additional capabilities such as product discovery, prioritisation, market analysis and product strategy.

That's a transition—not starting from zero.

Adjacency can work in many directions

  • Same role → new skills
  • Same skills → new role
  • Related role → new industry
  • Existing experience → new occupation

The better question

Instead of asking:

"What completely different career can I enter?"

ask:

"Which careers are close enough to my current skills that I can realistically move into them?"

Then identify:

Skills I already have + skills I need to acquire = transition plan

Career Diya takeaway

A good career switch is often not a leap.

It is a bridge.
