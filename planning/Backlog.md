## Core principles (deferred to real serve paths)

### Proficiency-truth

CareerĀsanā must never treat a user's self-assessment as ground truth, and should
systematically err toward assuming *less* proficiency than claimed.

**Rationale:** self-ratings are unreliable in a predictable direction — users overestimate
(Dunning-Kruger: low-to-moderate skill correlates with overconfidence). The errors are
asymmetric — over-preparing costs wasted time; under-preparing costs a failed interview, the
exact outcome the product exists to prevent. A good coach makes you prove readiness cheaply,
not take your word for it.

**Design implications when built:**
1. Intake asks for evidence ("what have you shipped/built/done with X?") not self-ratings
   ("rate your X 1-5") — evidence is concrete, checkable, Dunning-Kruger-resistant, and
   consistent with the Venture pillar and the anti-psychometric stance.
2. Unverified skills default low and are raised by evidence, biasing toward safe
   over-preparation.
3. Claimed-but-unverified skills get early lightweight verification tasks in the Blueprint
   ("confirm your Python is production-ready: build X"), so the real gap surfaces before the
   interview, not during it.
4. The Twin carries a claimed-vs-verified confidence dimension (partly exists via
   `basis`/`confidence`) so the whole system reasons about proficiency-truth consistently.

**Current state:** the empty-current-role "everything netNew" stand-in in the raw Blueprint UI
(`/blueprint`, `POST /blueprint/derive`) is a temporary fresh-start assumption for demoing the
interaction model, **not** the real skill model. Replace with evidence-based intake when
Blueprint gets its real serve path (`core.goal` creation + `promoteBlueprint` wiring — see the
serve-path item below).

### Intents don't overlap — they connect via explicit escape hatches

Each intent (career switch, career progression, career advice, etc.) stays single-purpose. Where
a user turns out to be in the wrong intent, the answer is an explicit one-way door routing them
to the right one — never accreting a partial version of intent B's capability into intent A.

**Established when:** "explore where this leads" (adjacent-role chips on the gap-analysis
result, `ActivationResult.adjacentRoles`) was built, then dropped as a user-facing feature. It
was an attempt to serve a confused user inside a flow built for someone who already knows their
destination, and every variant of it produced either decision-paralysis risk, unbounded
browsing, or analyses drifting from the user's real position (a demonstrated instance: chaining
`currentRole` off the hypothetical role being viewed rather than the user's actual recorded
position — wrong by construction, not just a rough edge). Confusion is a signal the user needs
the career-advice intent — which requires open-ended intake and analysis, not a dropdown or
multiselect — not a signal that the switch intent needs a discovery feature bolted on.

**How to apply:**
1. Escape-hatch CTAs appear on pre-commitment screens only — the activation input form, the
   gap-analysis result, and the compare-explorations grid — never past a commitment point (e.g.
   not on the relevant-experience checklist or task-identification result, which sit past "Set as
   Goal" and already have an exit via "← Back to your explorations").
2. Worded as an offer of help ("we can help with the harder question"), never as a prompt to
   second-guess ("are you sure?") — the former routes a genuinely confused user out; the latter
   plants doubt in a decision the product just helped a confident user make.
3. `adjacentRoles`/`toAdjacentRoleSuggestions` (contract v1.8.0, `start.ts`) stays live
   server-side even though unrendered — it's zero extra cost (already-loaded substrate data),
   and it's a plausible reasoning input for the career-advice intent once that ships, not a
   dead field to remove.

## Deferred decisions
- [ ] **Cache-personalization mismatch — bucket-level sharing reads as individual personalization**: `identification_cache` (migration 025) and `gap_analysis_cache` (migration 026) are both shared, role-pair-keyed caches by design — correct for cost, but the counsellor/gap-analysis prose can contain user-specific-sounding references (e.g. "your Scrum Master stint") that came from whichever user's request produced the cache-writing call. Every subsequent user landing on the same cache key (same role pair, and for identification, same gate bucket) gets that same prose verbatim, even though the prose reads as if it were written for them specifically. Confirmed live: identical inputs from two different accounts produced byte-identical output on a cache hit. Not a bug — the cache is working exactly as designed — but a conscious product decision needs to be made: either (a) accept bucket-level sharing as the deferred/premium-tier tradeoff it already implicitly is, and adjust prompt instructions to avoid first-person-sounding call-outs that don't generalize across users in the same bucket, or (b) narrow the cache key (e.g. add a coarse experience-summary bucket) at a real cost-vs-personalization tradeoff. Revisit once real usage data shows how often two different users actually land on the same bucket.

- [ ] **Unseeded role pairs now 500 instead of silently degrading (consequence of the Bug 1/2 fix)**: `startActivation` (`start.ts`) used to swallow a substrate-fetch failure and proceed with an ungrounded (and, pre-fix, sometimes literally empty) gap analysis. That silent-degradation path was removed — a substrate fetch that fails (e.g. a desired/current role not yet seeded, hitting the unimplemented `LiveSubstrateGenerator`) now propagates and the route returns a raw `500`. This is the correct direction (visible > hidden — see the "no response should look successful while empty" fix), but a raw 500 for "this role isn't in our catalog yet" is not a good end-user experience either — it should surface a meaningful, distinguishable "no data for this role yet" state instead of an opaque error. Needs the same treatment `/blueprint/identify`'s opaque-500 gap already needed. Don't build the nice error state before `LiveSubstrateGenerator` is real — the same fix (grounding arbitrary roles, not just the 25 seeded ones) resolves both the 500-instead-of-degrade sharpness AND the underlying capability gap. Sharpens the priority of `LiveSubstrateGenerator` (`substrate.ts` — currently `throw new Error('LiveSubstrateGenerator not yet implemented — use AI_MODE=mock')`): it is the next real build for arbitrary-role support, not a someday item.

- [ ] **Input design + product staging — constrain the funnel, not the engine (Apple-I stage decision)**: the relevant-experience, from-role, and to-role inputs are currently free-text; the decision is to move them toward guided/bounded inputs (dropdowns) while deliberately **preserving** a free-expression path, not eliminating it. **Chosen shape**: (a) from/to-role → searchable combobox over a canonical list (normalizes for caching + avoids typos; whether this caps the product at seeded roles vs. guides within an open set is entangled with the `LiveSubstrateGenerator` decision above — decide that deliberately, don't let "dropdown for caching" silently cap the product); (b) weekly hours → bounded input (cap ~40-60h, slider/number) — correctness only, has **zero** caching effect since weekly hours isn't in the identification/gap-analysis cache keys today; (c) relevant experience → dropdown (options ideally derived from / grounded in the to-role's O*NET substrate, reducing error for O*NET roles; non-O*NET roles more error-prone, "Other" catches overflow) → optional "would you like to elaborate?" Yes/No → free-text on Yes. **Contradiction rule (required)**: when both a dropdown selection AND free-text elaboration exist, inference uses ONLY the free-text and ignores the selection — avoids the user's own mis-selection poisoning the analysis. **Caching corollary (required, or it's a personalization-leak bug)**: the cache key must branch on elaboration — dropdown-only selections are shareable-cached (bounded, generic); free-text elaborations must be keyed-on-text OR treated as cache-miss-only, so free-text-derived personalized output never gets served to a later dropdown-only user in the same bucket (this is the same failure mode as the cache-personalization mismatch entry above, but self-inflicted rather than inherited — must not ship the dropdown redesign without this branch). **Anti-anchoring copy (required)**: dropdowns anchor users to the nearest listed option even when "Other" is more accurate, silently suppressing off-taxonomy signal — so make "Other" prominent/inviting and frame the elaboration prompt to invite the unexpected ("anything else about your background that might be relevant, even if it seems unrelated?").

  **Staging rationale (why constrain now)**: this is the Apple-I / GPT-1 stage — a shoestring startup can survive being limited but not being unreliable. Free-text maximizes richness and human-connect but admits bad signals (users don't know what's relevant — e.g. a SWE→PM candidate citing "created bug reports"), which costs both inference money and accuracy. Constrained inputs buy reliability-within-scope + cache efficiency + lower dropoff (a hesitant user picks "No" instead of freezing at a blank box) — exactly what an early product needs to earn trust and preserve runway. The expressive, higher-EQ version is a **later rung**, earned by the money, data, and validated demand this version generates — the constrained MVP is the instrument that reveals whether/how to build the rich version, not just thrift.

  **The hedge that keeps this from foreclosing the future** — constrain the **funnel** (UI/input layer, cheap to loosen later), not the **engine** (data/inference layer, expensive to rebuild): (1) keep the "Other → free-text" path live from day one even if rare, so the inference pipeline already handles free-text and expanding later is turning up a dial, not building a new capability; (2) capture raw input even when bucketing (storage is cheap, lost signal is unrecoverable — this is also what makes a future "Other-data improves the option sets" pipeline possible); (3) keep the inference/prompt layer capable of richer input than the UI currently sends (the counsellor prompt already handles free-text — don't dumb it down to match the constrained UI). Result: the richer version is a UI-and-dial change over infrastructure that already supports it, not a second product.

  **Deferred sub-item**: the "Other free-text improves the dropdown option sets over time" self-improving loop is a real engineering pipeline (collect → cluster → promote → redeploy), **not** a free property — it's future capability, not day-one. Day-one option sets must be genuinely good on their own (helped by O*NET grounding), not thin-but-self-healing. Also: AI-generated dropdown options are AI output and must be **validated**, not trusted — a plausible-wrong option is worse than a user's own wrong guess, because it carries the UI's authority.

  This is a decision + design record, not a build ticket — the actual input redesign (combobox, bounded hours input, dropdown+elaborate flow, cache-key branching) is future work.

- [ ] **Relevant-experience input redesign — substrate-derived multi-select + free-text, one screen, structured-context integration**: fully specifies the `(c) relevant experience` piece of the input-design decision above, superseding its dropdown-shape sketch. Replaces the current free-text `relevantExperience` field. **Structure** (one screen, optional/skippable): a soft umbrella header ("Anything you've done that connects to this role? (optional)") over two paths shown together, not sequenced — (1) a multi-select checklist whose options are **the to-role substrate's own skills** ("Which of these do you already have experience with?"), and (2) a free-text field always reachable on the same screen ("Or tell us about other relevant experience"). No upfront mode-choice (rejected — an extra decision-point taxes the hesitant users the design protects; list-default-with-visible-free-text achieves the same self-sorting in one step, no separate branch to navigate). **Why substrate-derived options**: dissolves the per-role option-curation burden (the options ARE the validated substrate — no separate content-authoring surface to maintain), scales to arbitrary roles for free (on-demand substrate generation, per substrate-generator v1, already produces them), can't misattribute (structured selections, unlike the false-attribution risk inherent to free-text — see the identification-cache-key fix), and pre-aligns the input with the delta taxonomy (`netNew`/`upskill`/`transferable`) the engine already reasons over. The free-text path exists specifically to catch off-taxonomy leverage the checkboxes structurally cannot (e.g. a wedding-planner's event-management experience for a PM switch — real leverage, no substrate skill to check). **No classifier** (rejected — bucketing free-text into categories reintroduces the exact false-attribution bug just fixed, and destroys the nuance that justifies having free-text at all; the counsellor reads free-text as text, not as a category). **Integration (Q1, decided — option (b))**: checkbox selections are passed to the counsellor as **structured context**, not a delta-modifier — the counsellor reasons "you indicated experience with X, Y" in its prose, but the computed gap-delta stays grounded purely in substrates. Avoids an over-claim risk: a user checking a box for a skill they don't really have would otherwise skip a real gap. The more powerful option (a) — user selections adjusting the computed delta itself, user-verified outranking inferred — is a deliberate future enhancement once over-claim protection exists, not v1. **Cache key (Q2, decided)**: branches on elaboration — `(promptVersion, fromRole, toRole, sorted-checked-skills, free-text-hash-if-present)`. Checkbox-only → keyed on the canonical sorted skill set (bounded by 2^N per role, good hit rate — this is the caching win); free-text present → key includes the raw-text hash (correct-but-less-cached, same tradeoff as the identification-cache fix, but opt-in here so it's minimized). This is the "cache key branches on elaboration" corollary from the input-design/staging decision above, made concrete — and it's what restores the cache efficiency that the false-attribution fix necessarily gave up (that fix keys every distinct free-text as a fresh generation; this redesign makes the common case checkbox-only again). **Copy framing** (durable logic; exact wording is user-testable later, not locked): affirming register — "which of these do you already have" — for the checkboxes (assumes competence, matches the checklist interface, removes the relevance-judgment burden since the checkboxes ARE the pre-judged-relevant skills); user-judgment register — "other experience you think is relevant?" — for the free-text (where the user's own relevance call is the point, since it's precisely for off-taxonomy experience the system can't pre-judge). Rejected "Do you have any experience you think is relevant" as the checkbox label — mismatches the checklist (asks "whether," the UI collects "which"), re-adds the relevance burden the checkboxes exist to remove, and "any" faintly primes deficiency.

- [ ] **O*NET grounding — planned as substrate-generator v2, not a separate project**: O*NET grounding is required, not optional-forever — an AI-inferred skill profile is a plausible guess; an O*NET-grounded one is anchored to real occupational data, and for a career product the accuracy of "what a role actually requires" is foundational. **Current state**: all 25 seeded substrates are `basis: inferred`, `source: ai_generated`; `knowledge.onet_occupation` is unseeded (see the "Seed knowledge.onet_occupation" item above), so the inferred/grounded distinction is currently **inert** — everything is model-inferred, nothing O*NET-crawl-verified. The `basis` field was designed for exactly this progression. **Plan**: O*NET grounding is v2 of the substrate generator, not a standalone build, because generation is the moment grounding is decided (generate-from-model-knowledge → inferred, vs. generate-anchored-to-O*NET → grounded). So: substrate-generator **v1** = knowledge-only (inferred), on-demand, validated, cached/seeded — unlocks arbitrary roles, extends exactly how the 25 seeded roles were made, and is what actually resolves `LiveSubstrateGenerator`'s current stub-throw. **v2** = O*NET-grounded — add the O*NET crawl/lookup pipeline seeding `knowledge.onet_occupation`, generation anchored to it, stamped `grounded`. The v1→v2 arc IS the inferred→grounded progression the schema already anticipates. **When**: v1 first (reach — arbitrary roles), v2 after v1 proves on-demand generation works (accuracy). **Reconciliation check (confirmed, story is coherent)**: the "ONET 15-2051.00 / grounded" seen on a `machine learning` gap item in live UI testing is **not** a real crawl-grounded substrate — it's `GapItem.confidence.basis` (`start.ts`'s gap-analysis prompt asks the model to self-label each skill `"stated" | "inferred" | "grounded"`, where `"grounded"` means "the model believes this is O*NET-verified"), a per-skill self-report the model makes inside its own JSON output, with zero actual O*NET lookup behind it. It is a different field, on a different object, from `knowledge.role_substrate.basis` (which every seeded row correctly reports as `inferred`, since no O*NET pipeline exists yet). No contradiction — just two same-named-but-unrelated `basis`/`grounded` concepts; worth not confusing the two once real O*NET grounding (v2) lands, since at that point `role_substrate.basis: 'grounded'` will mean something the model's own self-report never verified.

- [ ] **Role canonicalization — backend housekeeping only in release 1; feeding the serving path is deferred**: separately from the adjacent-roles/explore-similar-roles feature itself (which is already shipping in release 1 — see below), deduplication/canonicalization of `role_substrate` `role_key`s — identifying that two different literal strings (e.g. "captain of ship in indian merchant navy" vs "captain in indian merchant navy") refer to the same real-world role, to reduce redundant substrate generation and improve internal categorization — is its own periodic, backend-only process (embeddings and/or LLM-reasoned equivalence, whichever is most accurate, since it's not user-facing). Its output today is purely internal metadata (BI/reporting/data-hygiene: grouping near-duplicate `role_key`s under a canonical/O*NET-aligned label). Because this metadata does not feed the serving path or the adjacent-roles feature in release 1, it can be **fully automated with no human-review gate** — a wrong grouping only costs internal reporting accuracy, not user-facing correctness (in contrast to a hypothetical live/serving-path merge, which was considered and rejected: even a well-tuned automated merge risks silently serving a user analysis for the wrong role when two genuinely distinct roles — e.g. "product manager" vs "technical product manager" — are wrongly judged equivalent; the reputational cost of that failure mode at low volume outweighs the trivial generation-cost savings of deduplication, so canonicalization must never determine what a user is served).

  **Typeahead ships as a user-facing UX aid in release 1** (canonical-role-aware suggestions on the from/to role inputs) — purely additive, ignorable, no serving-path effect. Every request — whether the user accepts a typeahead suggestion or types a custom role — always resolves to an exact-match substrate for its own literal input. No merge/canonicalization logic ever sits in the serving path; a user's analysis is always grounded in the substrate for the exact role they specified. This invariant is what makes the rest of this decision safe.

  **Adjacent-roles / "explore similar roles" IS in scope for release 1** — but it is powered by each substrate's own `adjacent_roles` field (`SubstratePayloadV1.adjacent_roles`: `role_key`, `onet_code?`, `direction: lateral/step_up/step_down`), generated directly by the AI at substrate-generation time — already working today (confirmed live: "captain in indian merchant navy" generated chief officer, marine superintendent, harbour pilot, designated person ashore, marine surveyor as adjacent roles). This is **not** a cross-role deduplication/canonicalization process — it's the model reasoning about role-adjacency as part of normal, already-trusted substrate generation. It carries none of the merge risk discussed above and needs no additional gating to ship in release 1.

  **Explicit trigger to revisit**: the moment canonical-grouping metadata is used to influence adjacent-roles/explore-similar-roles output (e.g., deduping the adjacent-roles list against canonical groups, or broadening/filtering suggestions using canonical groups rather than each substrate's own AI-generated adjacency field), or any other user-facing serving decision — reintroduce a human-review gate (or a documented conservative-threshold decision) on the canonicalization step at that point. This is deferred to a later release, not release 1; explicitly parked, not solved.

- [ ] **Substrate verification — Type 1 (validate inline) vs. Type 2 (async review queue, not a blocking gate)**: a generated substrate is untrusted AI output written to `role_substrate` — shared reference data that grounds **every** transition involving that role, from- and to-side, so it's higher-stakes than a cache entry. Two failure modes, requiring different defenses. **Type 1 — structurally malformed / injected**: missing fields, bad enums, wrong skill count, model-invented `onet_code`, prompt-injection garbage. Fully detectable mechanically → caught by hard validation inline, before any write. Solved by the validator (port/harden `scripts/substrate/validate.mjs`, which already does exactly this and has been proven against all 25 seeded roles). **Type 2 — structurally valid but substantively wrong**: well-formed but lists the wrong skills, mis-rates criticality, misses a real skill. **Not** mechanically detectable — detecting it requires already knowing the right answer, which is the thing generation produces; it needs domain judgment (human or another model). **Key consequence**: you cannot have "on-demand AND substantively-verified" — substantive verification needs a human, which is a bottleneck that breaks on-demand (that bottleneck is exactly what the existing offline generate→review→commit pipeline, `scripts/substrate/generate.mjs` + `commit.mjs`, already is). **Decision for v1**: ship on-demand generation with bulletproof Type-1 validation, write inline, stamp `source: ai_generated` / `basis: inferred` (marking it as unreviewed AI output — the schema already carries this distinction, nothing new needed). Accept that Type 2 (plausible-but-wrong) can slip through — tolerable because it's plausible (not garbage), correctable (regenerate/fix the substrate → retroactively improves everyone via the shared cache), bounded (one role), and vastly better than a 500 for an arbitrary role. **Do not** build a synchronous staging-table-then-promote gate — as a human gate it breaks on-demand; as an automated gate it's redundant with inline validation, since nothing automated catches Type 2 anyway. **Backlog (build later)**: an async, non-blocking review surface — review/correct accumulated `ai_generated` substrates after they're live (trust-then-verify), correcting the shared reference retroactively. This is the "staging table" instinct repositioned as a post-hoc review queue, not a pre-write holding pen. Build when there are enough generated substrates to warrant the tooling *and* usage shows Type 2 errors are frequent enough to matter — not day one. The real v1 Type-2 mitigation is simply: use a capable-enough model that common roles are usually right, and validate hard so garbage never lands.

- [ ] **Don't pre-warm the seeded-role cache grid — lazy demand-fill is the design**: decision is to **not** proactively pre-generate/warm `identification_cache` or `gap_analysis_cache` for the 25-role switch grid. **Rationale**: (a) "warm end-to-end" is per-pair (600 switch pairs across 25 roles), and identification is per-pair-**per-gate-bucket** (thousands of cells) — pre-warming is real money (~$54+ for one bucket per pair, more with buckets) spent speculatively on cells users may never hit; (b) the cache is *designed* to lazy-fill — the first real user to request a pair pays ~9¢ (1¢ gap analysis + 8¢ identification) to warm it for everyone after, which targets spend at real demand instead of guessed demand; (c) prompts are still v1 — a `prompt_version` bump invalidates the entire pre-warmed grid (both caches key on it), so pre-warming before prompt-stability wastes the spend outright. The 25 seeded roles are already in the state that matters: substrate-safe (no 500s), warm-on-first-real-use. "Not warm" only means "first user waits ~30s + ~9¢, then it's warm for all" — acceptable, and shouldn't be pre-paid. **If proactive warming is ever wanted**: seed only the ~10-15 obviously-common/demo pairs, and only *after* prompt v1 is locked (~$1-2, defensible) — never the full grid. Let real/beta usage warm what matters.

- [ ] **To-role dropdown is intent-scoped — switcher excludes same-role progressions**: the career switcher's to-role options must exclude same-role seniority progressions (e.g. `data analyst → senior data analyst`), which are a *different* intent (career progression / grow-in-role, per the intent-architecture decision above), not a switch. A switcher offers different roles, not seniority levels of the current one. Surfaced by the stale `data analyst → senior data analyst` `identification_cache` row, which is doubly irrelevant to the switcher: wrong mode (mock-written — see the cache-coherence note below) *and* wrong intent. So: the to-role dropdown is scoped by the selected intent; the switcher's list is "roles you could switch **to**," excluding progressions of the from-role. Depends on the multi-intent UI landing (see the intent-architecture decision above) to have an "intent" to scope by; until then this is a design constraint to keep in mind, not yet enforceable.

  **Cache-coherence awareness note** (small, from the same investigation): `identification_cache` isn't coupled to substrate existence — mock-mode runs can write identification-cache rows for pairs whose substrates aren't seeded (e.g. `data analyst → senior data analyst`, confirmed not in the 25 seeded `role_substrate` roles); such rows 500 on substrate fetch before the cache is ever reached in live mode (harmless-but-unreachable, not a live-mode-serving bug). Mostly resolves itself once substrate-generator v1 lands (arbitrary roles become fetchable, so cached pairs stop being substrate-orphaned). Awareness item; consider a cleanup/coherence check (e.g. a periodic query joining cache role-keys against seeded substrates) if it starts to matter operationally.

- [ ] **Intent architecture — multi-intent entry, seed-not-lock, blank-slate recommender (decided via first-principles + adversarial review)**: **Layers** (kept distinct, reinforcing not competing) — architecture/identity: CareerĀsanā IS a career operating system (memory-first, event-driven — Twin, substrates, blueprints, event log, longitudinal state); this is the engine/moat, unchanged. Solutioning/behavior: reasons and presents like a pro career counsellor (counsellor-assessment design) — "pocket/24-7 advisor" is the lens for HOW it responds. Positioning/marketing: likely "Your 24/7 career advisor" (broader mass appeal than "career operating system," which is accurate but abstract) — final copy is a later marketing decision, noted not locked; don't let the marketing label erode the OS architectural ambition, since the OS is what makes the advisor persistent and grounded. **Entry**: presents an intent selection — narrows the AI's target (better grounding/accuracy, fewer hallucinations), shrinks the permutation space (better caching/cost), validates the user's felt-certainty ("this app gets me"). **Intents**: switch careers / grow in current role / return to work / side career / blank-slate (from-role-known, to-role-unknown) / others TBD — NOT separate modules; most are the SAME engine (situation→gap→counsellor-assessment→plan) with different framing and from/to semantics. The one architectural outlier is blank-slate: from-role known (student/current role), to-role unknown, inverting the first step into a to-role fit-recommender (suggests fitting to-roles from from-role + situation) that then feeds the shared engine once the user picks a suggested to-role — even the outlier is shared engine + a recommender front-step, reinforcing one-engine-many-entries. **Three constraints** (each earned against a failure mode): (1) a blank-slate/escape option is mandatory — protects confused-but-high-need users from self-misdiagnosis; (2) intent seeds, doesn't lock — mechanism: a pure-selection flow gives the AI no signal to detect a wrong intent, so instead of "AI revises mid-reasoning," commit intent → produce assessment → offer post-output reconsideration ("after reading this, looking for something else?" → show other intents) — confusion caught at two moments, entry-time (escape option, pre-aware) and output-time (reconsideration, post-aware); (3) output is nuanced-in-reasoning, simple-in-presentation (counsellor structure) — solves overwhelm-churn by packaging nuance, not reducing it. **Iterative confusion-handling flow** (blank-slate/reconsideration path): probe → AI infer → "is it one of these intents?" → "still not sure" → re-probe → infer again → cap at 3 iterations → then nudge "let's just try one and see how it feels" (anti-analysis-paralysis; low-risk because seed-not-lock means trying one isn't permanent). **Blank-slate intent label** — criteria locked, copy not: plain > clever, concrete > idiomatic, self-explanatory to functional-English/tier-2 users (India-first), distinct from "choose a career," minimal deficit-admission (stigma suppresses clicks, defeating the escape hatch). Rejected: "Not sure" (stigma), "Explore my options"/"Explore career options" (subject-ambiguity / collides with switch-choose), "Show me what fits" ("fits" idiom), "Help me choose" (collides with chooser, indirect meaning fails). Finalists to user-test: "I am open to ideas" (best on zero-stigma/click-willingness), "Where can I go from here?" (explicit + encodes from-role), "Suggest careers for me" (mechanism-accurate) — final copy via tier-2 user testing at build. **Sequencing**: finish the intelligent switcher first (shared engine); intent can seed the switcher prompt now as a grounding input; full multi-intent UI + blank-slate recommender = later builds.
